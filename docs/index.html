Yantra Nadi यंत्र नाडी

Pulse diagnosis for machines.

A clip-on vibration sensor that tells a mechanic which part of a generator, pump or vehicle is wearing out, days before it fails. It learns each machine's own normal in its first day, names the failing part from the bearing's geometry, and works without the internet.

Try it on a machine near you

Your phone's microphone stands in for the vibration sensor. Hold it near a running fan, fridge or pump. It listens for ten seconds to learn what normal sounds like, then watches for change. Tuck a strip of paper so it brushes the fan blade and watch it flag the change, and the band it came from.

Replay a bearing running to failure

On NASA's IMS rig, four bearings shared one shaft under a 2,700 kg load until one of them failed, with one second of vibration recorded every ten minutes. Drag through a run. The system saw only the first 24 hours of each bearing before it started judging, and was never shown a failure.

Why it can name the part

A spall on an outer race is struck by each roller in turn. With 16 rollers in a row at 2,000 rpm, the ZA-2115 bearing's geometry puts that at 236.4 strikes a second. The envelope spectrum of bearing 1 in run 2 shows that line, and its double, once the damage starts. Inner-race and ball defects would sit at 296.9 Hz and 279.8 Hz instead.

Naming test: 52 recordings with seeded faults

Case Western Reserve University cut faults of 0.18 to 0.53 mm into test bearings and recorded them at four loads. Each square below is one recording. The system names the part only when one defect line stands at least 10 dB clear of its neighbourhood. Otherwise it declines: sending a mechanic to the wrong part costs a day, and saying "inspect, part not identified" costs nothing.

How it works

01 · Learn

For its first day on a machine the node records one second of vibration every ten minutes and builds that machine's own normal. It needs no failure data from anywhere else.

02 · Watch

Each new second is compared with that normal: overall level, how spiky the signal is, and the strength of each defect line. A warning needs three readings in a row, so a door slam does not page anyone.

03 · Name

The asset register holds the bearing number at each point. From its geometry the node knows where an outer race, inner race or ball defect will show, and looks there. The mechanic is told the part and its spare number.

The node, as designed

ESP32-S3 microcontroller and ST's IIS3DWB vibration accelerometer, flat to about 6 kHz, which is the bandwidth every result on this page was computed within. Sealed metal housing on a magnet or stud mount, powered from the machine's own 12/24 V battery with a backup cell. About ₹4,000 a point at one-off retail prices (estimate).

Why offline

The health of a security force's vehicles and generators is operational information. Most commercial condition-monitoring runs through a vendor's cloud. Yantra Nadi never needs a connection outside the premises.

Proposed pilot

Six months with one Maharashtra Police Motor Transport section, about 60 measuring points. Generators and pumps first, where the method above applies directly. Vehicles second, measured at idle, when the engine and everything belted to it turns at a steady speed.

What is proven, and what is not

  • Proven: the method, on two public laboratory datasets, with the numbers above.
  • Not yet: the hardware node (bench build from November 2026), any data from a police or forces machine, and vehicle drivelines at changing speed.
  • How the rules were set: the alarm thresholds and the band search were fixed while looking at IMS run 2 and the Case Western set. IMS runs 1 and 3 were then scored once, with nothing changed.
  • Healthy-bearing alarms: eight healthy bearings did alarm late in their runs, every one after the failing bearing had already warned, and in every case the failing bearing's sensor was the loudest on the shaft at that moment. On one machine the system reports the loudest point as the source.
  • Code and data: github.com/amayakavya/yantra-nadi. The analysis reruns from the public datasets in about six minutes on a laptop.
